Ordinary quantum mechanics describes a fixed set of particles: one electron in a hydrogen atom, or two in helium. Its wavefunction $\psi(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_N)$ has one slot for each particle, and the number of slots never changes. For atoms that works very well.

Special relativity breaks it. Einstein's $E = mc^2$ says that mass is a form of energy, so with enough energy, new particles can appear, and particles can vanish into energy. Once that can happen, "the wavefunction of $N$ particles" stops being a description of anything: $N$ itself changes.

## Particles that appear and disappear

A gamma-ray photon passing close to an atomic nucleus can turn into an electron and a positron, its antiparticle. The photon needs at least the two particles' rest energy:

$$
E_\gamma \ge 2 m_e c^2 \approx 1.022\ \text{MeV}
$$

The reverse happens too: an electron meeting a positron can vanish, leaving two photons. Neither process fits a theory with a fixed list of particles, and both are seen every day, in particle detectors and in hospital PET scanners.

## Pinning a particle down makes more of them

Relativity also limits how sharply one particle can be located. By the uncertainty principle, squeezing an electron into a region of size $\Delta x$ gives it momentum, and so energy, of about $\hbar c / \Delta x$. Once that reaches $2 m_e c^2$, there is enough energy to make a new electron–positron pair. That happens at about the electron's Compton wavelength,

$$
\lambda_C = \frac{h}{m_e c} \approx 2.43\ \text{pm},
$$

so below that scale, "one electron, here" is no longer a well-posed question: try to look that closely, and you may find three.

## What fields give us

A field is something with a value at every point of space, at every moment, as the electric field is. If every particle is a ripple in a field, the problems above go away:

- **The number of particles can change.** Ripples can be made and absorbed; the field itself is always there.
- **Every electron is identical.** Two ripples in the same field can't differ, which is why all electrons have exactly the same mass and charge.
- **Nothing acts at a distance.** A field changes only where it is pushed, and the change spreads no faster than light.
