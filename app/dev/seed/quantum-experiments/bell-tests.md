[[id:1oye7a74fnmnb#bells-theorem|Bell's theorem]] turned a philosophical argument into a number to measure: if $S$ comes out above 2, no theory with local hidden variables can explain the result.

| Year | Experiment | What it added |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1972 | Freedman and Clauser | The first test, with pairs of photons from calcium atoms |
| 1982 | Aspect, Dalibard and Roger | Measurement settings switched while the photons were in flight |
| 1998 | Weihs and colleagues | Settings chosen at random, too far apart for any signal to link them |
| 2015 | Delft, Vienna and NIST | The first tests with no loopholes left open at once |

Every test has found the quantum prediction. The 2015 Delft experiment measured $S = 2.42 \pm 0.20$ with electron spins 1.3 km apart. In 2022 Alain Aspect, John Clauser and Anton Zeilinger shared the Nobel Prize in Physics for this line of work.

## The loopholes

- **Locality.** If one side's setting could reach the other before its measurement finished, a hidden signal could fake the correlation. Choosing the settings quickly, far apart, closes it.
- **Detection.** If most particles go undetected, the ones that are detected might not be a fair sample. Detecting nearly every pair closes it.
- **Freedom of choice.** The settings must not be correlated with any hidden variables. The 2018 Big Bell Test took its settings from choices made by about 100,000 people playing an online game.
