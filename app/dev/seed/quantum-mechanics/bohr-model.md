In 1913 Niels Bohr explained the spectrum of hydrogen by letting its electron circle the nucleus only in orbits whose angular momentum is a whole multiple of $\hbar$. Those orbits have energies

$$
E_n = -\frac{13.6\ \text{eV}}{n^2}, \qquad n = 1, 2, 3, \dots
$$

and the atom gives off light when the electron drops from one orbit to a lower one, with the energy difference carried by one photon.

The model got hydrogen's spectrum right, but it failed for every atom with more than one electron, and it couldn't say why the allowed orbits were allowed. [[id:0rmiavdb9vpd6|The Schrödinger equation]] answers both, and gives the same energies for hydrogen.
